# MedClub V0.7 design notes

## Direction

V0.7 treats the store as a **clinical supply shelf**, not a generic ecommerce template. Deep medical red and russet frame calm warm surfaces. Product cards use rounded asymmetric shapes, layered specimen-like labels and controlled scan-line responses instead of hard rectangular tiles.

The product visuals are intentionally icon-led because verified photography was not supplied. They create a distinctive visual system without presenting invented merchandise images as real products.

## Motion language

- Hover and keyboard focus send a short scanning line through a product card.
- Product objects subtly settle and orbit instead of floating continuously.
- Store-tab icons animate on hover and remain visibly energized while active.
- Page transitions reuse the established clinical pulse/signal language.
- Reservation progress is directional and restrained, emphasizing state rather than decoration.

`prefers-reduced-motion` removes decorative scanning, orbit and transition movement while preserving every label, status and action.

## Information hierarchy

Catalog cards lead with product identity, category, stock and price. Detail pages move variant and quantity choices beside the product context. The cart keeps line-level options editable and places the reservation summary in a clear sticky tray on wide screens.

My Orders belongs to the personal workspace. It leads with status and next action, then shows item snapshots, collection details and totals. Cancellation appears only when the current state allows it.

## Trust boundaries

- Products, prices, stock and collection details are visibly illustrative.
- The CTA says reserve for collection; it never claims a payment was made.
- No card or other payment fields exist.
- Order totals are deterministic snapshots, but browser storage is not financial authority.
- Real product photography and supplier claims must be verified before launch.

## Responsive and RTL behavior

Desktop retains the collapsible vertical rail and optional horizontal preference. Phones retain horizontal navigation. The asymmetric product grid collapses without changing semantic order. Logical CSS properties and bilingual messages support RTL, while buttons and status paths preserve a predictable reading sequence.

## Future API handoff

The screens communicate the intended customer flow but never claim server authority. Real-time inventory, reservations, checkout, fulfillment, refunds, financial reporting and notifications must come from authenticated services. The current local adapter is preview infrastructure only.
